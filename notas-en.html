Notes · Photographic gaze

Notebook of seeing


Seeking the simple, what moves us, nature, people, spirituality and the details others don't see.

Note I

Learning to see before photographing


A creative course works best around an idea, not around phone buttons. The photographic eye: training attention and finding beauty in the everyday. Light and atmosphere: how a scene changes with the hour and the emotion. Intuitive composition: balance, emptiness, colours, lines, textures. Visual narrative: creating a story with 5–10 images. Sensitive editing: not transforming reality, but enhancing it.

Through my background in art direction, decoration and photography, my differential value is not teaching technique: it is teaching a way of seeing.

Note II

Photographic walks


Walk "Hidden beauty". Two hours on foot. Exercises: photographing a shadow. Finding three textures. Taking a portrait without asking for a pose. Capturing an instant of silence.

Walk "Invisible Barcelona". Looking at doors, flowers, walls, reflections, people. Ending with a small selection of images and a conversation about the gaze.

And a second line very much mine: photographic experiences in special places —Formentera, Bali, Capri— blending photography, nature, calm and sensitivity.


Note III

What makes me different


My photographs don't seek perfection; they seek to awaken something in whoever looks at them. I am drawn to the beauty of the simple: a gaze, an honest smile, flower petals, a calm landscape. What, being everyday, we often stop seeing.

In a world full of noise and fleeting images, I try for my photographs to be an invitation to pause. A space of peace, beauty and authenticity that reconnects us with the essential.


"To observe is not to capture the light, but to wait for objects to consent to reveal their memory."
